## 1. What the game is

**GLASS CITY** is a GTA-style open-world game set in a near 1:1 recreation of **Toledo, Ohio** and its suburbs.
It is the owner's passion project for his own city.
- **Owner / creative lead:** Jay, a student. He decides names, story and direction. He will write the story and do the voice acting himself.
- **Builder:** an AI coding assistant (Claude) writes the code, data pipeline and models in small tasks. The owner uses ChatGPT as a "creative director" to plan; the plan lives in `docs/GLASS_CITY_Development_Bible_v1.0.pdf`.
- **Engine:** **Godot 4.3** (GDScript) now. A full visual rebuild in **Unreal Engine 5** is planned later, so the data pipeline is engine-neutral.
- **Owner's PC:** GTX 1080 Ti (11 GB), the reference machine for performance budgets.
- **Tone and genre:** GTA IV/V-style life sim and crime sandbox: driving, on-foot, fighting, guns, police, jobs, businesses, homes, needs. **No missions yet**; the owner said to build the world and systems first.

### Art direction: Bully style on modern geometry (owner decision, 2026-11-21)
- The owner wants **"4K upscaled Bully" gameplay looks, with modern polygon counts on models**. Bully: Scholarship Edition's art style:
  - hand-painted-looking, clean textures;
  - warm, soft, sunny light with gentle bloom;
  - slightly caricatured people (bigger heads and hands, strong features).
- The city geometry stays modern-detailed and 1:1 accurate; only surfaces, lighting and characters change.
- **Not Agefield High** (owner, 2026-11-21). Agefield High: Rock the School (Refugium Games, UE5, 2026) is a poorly received Bully-inspired game with a generic, clean, plastic store-asset look and empty streets. GLASS CITY must look hand-crafted and lived-in instead:
  - textures keep their grime, stains and weathering;
  - streets are full: parked cars, props, people;
  - lighting is warm but keeps shadow depth;
  - yards are real and patchy, not perfect carpets.
- **Rejected:** a full-screen "painted"/oil filter. The owner never asked for that. The look comes from the art, not a screen filter.
- **How it works:**
  - `source_data/look.json` `"style": "bully"` (default; `--look=classic` gives the old look) drives `scripts/look.gd`;
  - colour textures load from `textures_bully/`, made by `tools/pipeline/make_bully_textures.py`: a light edge-preserving pass that tames photo grain but keeps the grime (30% of the original detail blended back), plus seamless large soft stains so nothing looks factory-clean; a warmer, richer palette; healthy but uneven green grass;
  - normal maps at 0.45 strength;
  - lighting preset in `main.gd` / `time_of_day.gd`: filmic tonemap, saturation 1.12, soft bloom, warm sun, 35% more ambient, softer shadow edges (sun angular size 0.9; 1.6 was too slow on the CPU renderer);
  - characters: the stylized mode of the character creator (section 7);
  - **3D trees near the camera:** chunks whose centre is within 240 m show the Blender trees as real 3D meshes (`assets/models/bully_tree_a/c.glb`). Billboards are used further out. `city_streamer._update_tree_lod` swaps them per chunk twice a second.
    - Bark is dark matte; leaves are alpha-tested and lit through.
    - About 2k triangles per tree; the leaf count may need trimming on low-end PCs.
  - **Lived-in house windows** (`shaders/window_interior.gdshader`, swapped onto the `hp_glass` material of every `hp_*` house model by `city_streamer._window_mat()`):
    - each pane picks its own treatment from a hash of its world position: curtains (6 colours; open, half-open or shut; tied back; valance), blinds (white, beige or yellowed, pulled to a random height, the odd crooked slat), a bedsheet or flag, or bare glass;
    - behind it is a fake room with real parallax: back wall, side walls, floor and ceiling, plus a couch or dresser shape, a picture and a doorway;
    - on top: a glass fresnel sky tint, a painted streak, and grimy corners;
    - at night, `occupancy` (from `time_of_day.gd`) picks which rooms are lit: warm light (a few TV-blue) glows through curtains and blinds;
    - pane UVs come from `pane_uv()` in `tools/blender/house_parts.py`. The old modelled blind strip is gone.
  - **Utility poles and overhead wires** (lived-in streets; Toledo neighbourhoods have them along nearly every alley):
    - `tools/pipeline/build_city.py` `utility_line()` puts a wooden pole about every 38 m along every OSM alley (`highway=service, service=alley`), 0.6 m off the edge;
    - along one side of residential streets (opposite the lamps) too, except where an alley runs within 45 m, because then the lines live in the alley;
    - models from `tools/blender/util_poles.py`: `util_pole`, `util_pole_t` (transformer can, 22%) and `util_pole_f` (flyers stapled on, 10%). Each has a cross-arm with insulators, a secondary rack, a cable clamp, step bolts and a pole tag;
    - chunk props `["wire:<y0>:<y1>", x0, z0, x1, z1]` link consecutive poles; the pole base heights (chunk ground) ride in the kind string, because the runtime terrain grid can sit metres below the chunk ground. `scripts/utility_wires.gd` turns each into five sagging cables (3 primaries, the triplex secondary and a phone/cable line) as one thin-tube mesh per chunk, with terrain heights at runtime;
    - tests: `utility_poles_and_wires`, `house_windows_lived_in`.
  - **Road wear and street hardware:**
    - `ground_pbr.gdshader` `wear` (on for the asphalt in Bully look): rotated repair patches with tar rims (about 1 in 6 cells of 7 m), alligator cracking in tired zones (voronoi edges masked by slow noise), glossy tar snakes, and oil blots, all in world space;
    - the same shader with `wear_mode = 1` on sidewalks: hairline cracks, weeds in some cracks, black gum spots and dirt drifts;
    - cast-iron `manhole` covers near the centreline every 72 m, and `storm_grate` drains at both curbs every 55 m (`util_poles.py`; build job `mdl`, kept only where they land on the road surface, 0.08 m road height).
  - **Back-yard fences** (`build_city.py` `yard_fence()` / `emit_fence()`): about 80% of houses get one, 55% chain-link and 25% wood privacy.
    - The fence leaves each side wall 35–55% of the way back, runs out to half the gap to the neighbour (probed against the other buildings), back to the alley (or about 10 m past the house) and across.
    - It's clipped per region off streets, sidewalks, walks, water and buildings. A garage breaks the fence; the gap at the house wall is the gate.
    - Drawn as instanced 2.4 m panels `fence_chain` / `fence_wood` (`util_poles.py fence_panel`, textures from `tools/pipeline/make_fence_textures.py`), so they cost no chunk geometry. Building them as chunk geometry doubled the vertex count, so that approach was dropped.
  - **Trash and clutter** (owner: "like real Toledo, every section about 5 variants, on sidewalks and grass near streets and walkways"). Models come from `tools/blender/clutter.py`, five per kind of place:
    - litter clusters `litter_a`–`e`: cans, cups, chip bags, bottles, butts, napkins, newspaper, flyers;
    - residential bulk trash `res_couch`, `res_mattress`, `res_tv`, `res_bags`, `res_chair`;
    - alley junk `alley_tires`, `alley_pallet`, `alley_bags`, `alley_mattress`, `alley_junk`;
    - business strips `com_boxes`, `com_cart`, `com_news`, `com_drink`, `com_bags`;
    - parking lots `lot_cart`, `lot_cart_tipped`, `lot_tire`, `lot_cone`, `lot_litter`;
    - industrial yards `ind_pallets`, `ind_drums`, `ind_tires`, `ind_scrap`, `ind_crates`.

    Placement (`build_city.py clutter_along()`, job `clut`):
    - streets: every 5.5 m on business streets (75% chance) and every 8 m on residential streets (55%), split between the gutter (on the road), the sidewalk and the grass strip or yard edge;
    - bulk trash at the residential curb (16% of grass-strip spots);
    - alleys every 10 m (50%), and beside footpaths every 10 m (35%);
    - parking lots one item per 260 m², industrial yards one per 420 m²;
    - an item must land on the road (gutter) or off it, as intended, and never inside a building.
  - **Toledo's 200 most-driven vehicles** (owner, 2026-11-21: "study Toledo traffic and look at the 200 most driven cars"): `source_data/toledo_fleet.json`, made by `tools/pipeline/make_toledo_fleet.py`.
    - It lists model and generation, a GLASS CITY body family, and a weight that is a share of the road per 1000.
    - It is an **estimate**: no public per-model registration counts exist for Lucas County. It is built from:
      - the Toledo used-car market;
      - Ohio DMV data (Chevrolet the top make, Silverado 1500 3.9%);
      - US vehicles in operation (F-150 3.7%, Silverado 2.8%, Ram 1500 1.3%; average age 12.8 years);
      - Toledo new-car sales;
      - local factors: Jeep's Toledo plant, domestic-brand loyalty, Chrysler minivans.
    - **Second-hand cars included** (owner: "I'm talking about second-hand cars too"): weights are re-balanced so the model-year mix matches what is actually driven, not new sales: 2022+ 15%, 2017–21 23%, 2012–16 25%, 2007–11 19%, 2002–06 11%, 2001 and older 7%. That's a little older than the US fleet (average age 12.8 years), for Toledo.
      - **Owner's observation (2026-11-21): "driving around Toledo it is mostly 2013 to 2003 cars".** The target is now 2022+ 5%, 2017–21 10%, 2014–16 13%, 2008–13 36%, 2003–07 26%, 2002 and older 10%. The fleet comes out about 65% model years 2003–2013. Added more 2003–2013 cars: '05–'09 Equinox, Torrent, Vue, Rendezvous, Aveo, G5, Five Hundred, Sable, Mariner, Mountaineer, Stratus, Pacifica wagon, Avalanche, Santa Fe, Spectra, Maxima, '03–'08 Corolla, '01–'05 Civic, Accent, Rio, Commander, Magnum, Colorado, Envoy, Town Car, DTS, Highlander, Murano, Element and Tribute.
      - **Off-brand names chosen** (owner: "pick all of them"): `tools/pipeline/fleet_names.py` → `parody_make` / `parody_model` in `toledo_fleet.json`. Makes:
        - Chevrolet→Corvane, Ford→Halden, Jeep→Overmark, Dodge→Swerve, Toyota→Takumi, Honda→Hanzo, Chrysler→Kreisler, Nissan→Nasso;
        - Buick→Bewick, GMC→GMT, Hyundai→Pellam, Pontiac→Pontier, Ram (and the old Dodge Rams)→Bulward, Kia→Kaiya, Mercury→Mercurio, Cadillac→Cavalcade;
        - Lincoln→Lancaster, Saturn→Satellite, Subaru→Subara, Mazda→Mazura, VW→Volksmobil, Oldsmobile→Oldham, Plymouth→Plymore.

        All 139 models have their own name, for example Silverado→Stockman 1500, Impala→Dutchess, F-150→Workhorse 150, Grand Caravan→Grand Convoy, Liberty→Freedom, Grand Cherokee→Grand Ridgeback, Cobalt→Garnet, PT Cruiser→PB Cruisette.
      - **Off-brand names are worked out with the owner** in the "GLASS CITY – Toledo's 200 Cars" Sheet artifact. Its "Brands" tab maps each real make to a parody make; brands already in the game are prefilled (Corvane, Halden, Overmark, Takumi, Hanzo, Pellam, Bulward). The "Cars" tab has an off-brand model column to fill in. When it's done, the names get read back into `toledo_fleet.json`.
      - Old beaters are in the list too: 1999–2006 Silverado, '97–'03 F-150, Jeep XJ Cherokee, Liberty, Impala, Grand Marquis, LeSabre, PT Cruiser, Windstar, Astro and more.
      - The boost per age bucket is capped at 2.2×, so a thin bucket can't vault one model to #1.
      - `year_weights` spreads each generation over its model years. At runtime the year will drive how worn the car is (fade, rust, dents, mismatched panels).
    - Body mix: about 39% SUVs and crossovers, 35% cars, 21% pickups, 5% vans. That's close to Ohio's registrations (UVs 40%, cars 33.5%, pickups 18.4%, vans 6.3%).
    - In-game every vehicle gets a parody brand and name (no real names). The owner picks the brand names.
  - **Toledo traffic study** (owner: "look at the news, random videos, analyze real traffic"): `source_data/toledo_traffic_study.json`.
    - Facts:

      - Toledo traffic deaths in 2025: 24.
      - 30 of 51 Lucas County fatal crashes in 2023 were OVI-related.
      - Crash weather in Toledo: 81% clear, about 10% rain, 2% snow.
      - Residents complain about neighbourhood speeding.
      - I-75 rush-hour backups at the DiSalle Bridge; I-475 widening.
    - The 25 most crash-prone intersections (Airport and Byrne first).
    - I couldn't watch videos; the study uses written news and public data.
    - In the game (`source_data/traffic.json`):
      - `driver_styles`: every car gets calm, rushed, reckless, elderly or impaired (impaired mostly 22:00–04:00). The style changes speed and following distance. Rushed and reckless drivers push yellows, sometimes run a fresh red (decided once per light) and roll stop signs.
      - `day_of_week`: Friday busiest, Sunday quietest.
      - Tests pin their own test cars to `calm`. New test: `traffic_driver_styles`.
  - **Lived-in cars** (`shaders/car_paint.gdshader`): road grime creeps up the lower body, the clear coat dulls there, and the lower glass is dusty. About 28% of cars (seeded by paint colour, so a car keeps its look while it drives) are faded beaters with rust round the arches and rockers.
  - **Bully trees:** `tools/blender/bully_trees.py` builds round and oval trees: a thick trunk and branches, and crowns of painted leaf-cluster cards with outward normals.
    - They are baked to billboards with `tools/dbg/bake_impostor.gd`, then cropped into `textures_bully/trees/` (same names plus `meta.json`). They replace the photo-scan tree cutouts automatically.
  - **Parked cars:** `scripts/parked_cars.gd`. Cars line residential (class 5) and tertiary (class 4) streets that have sidewalks, within 170 m of the camera.
    - Spots are hashed from each street's node ids, so the same cars are there every visit.
    - 7.5 m slots, 42% filled, none within 11 m of a corner.
    - Roster models and paints; static colliders on layer 2.
    - `--no-parked` turns it off. Test: `parked_cars_line_side_streets`.
  - **Yard life** (`build_city.yard_life`, models from `tools/blender/yard_props.py`): `YARD_LIFE=0` turns it off.
    - Foundation shrubs along house fronts (75% of houses).
    - Two city trash carts beside the house (70%).
    - Patio chairs on full porches (45%).
    - Now and then a bike by the steps, a grill or a kids' slide in the side yard.
    - All deterministic per house.
  - **Trash carts:** three colours (`yard_toter` green and `yard_toter_k` black for trash, `yard_toter_b` blue for recycling). Rounded bodies, lids, wheels, handles.
  - **Signs** (jobs, homes, places) stand on the ground under them (`services._ground_y` ignores marquees and awnings). The cinema sign used to sit on top of the EASTGATE marquee.
  - **Tree crowns** are lit through by the sun (backlight), so they don't go black against the light.
  - **Grass:** finer blades, 14 per clump, 6–13 cm.
  - **Grime band:** a 1 m dirt and splash-back strip (`textures/grime_base.png`, transparent) along the bottom of every house wall.
  - **Bark:** 3D tree trunks use `textures/bark_painted.png` (triplanar).
  - `ModelLib` makes textured see-through cards on any model alpha-tested.
  - **Scenery shots** (`@cam`) hide passers-by within 12 m of the camera.
  - **3D grass** (owner, 2026-11-21): `scripts/grass.gd` + `shaders/grass.gdshader`. Real blade clumps (9 curved blades each) on every lawn and grass area within 46 m of the camera.
    - Grass is split into 8 m cells per chunk and scattered area-weighted with a fixed seed: 7 clumps/m² close up, 2.2 at the edge, capped at 520 per cell.
    - Blades sway in a gusty wind, shrink into the ground at the radius edge (no popping), and take snow.
    - Instanced (MultiMesh), no shadows. `--no-grass` turns it off. Test: `grass_grows_on_lawns`.

## 3. Running and testing

- Open `project.godot` in **Godot 4.3** and press F5. `data/` (city chunks) is generated and not in git; build it with `tools/build.sh city`.
- **Tests:** `GODOT=/path/to/godot sh tools/run_tests.sh`. This runs:
  - Python data tests;
  - a compile check of every script;
  - 58 in-game headless tests.
  
  **All 58 pass** as of 2026-11-21. One test: `godot --headless --path . -- --test=res://tests/out/r.json --only=<substring>`.
- **Screenshots:** `godot --path . --rendering-driver vulkan --resolution 1280x720 -- --demo=shots --out=DIR --shotlist=FILE.json`
  - Shotlist entries: `["@cam", [x, y, z], [look_x, look_y, look_z], "name"]`, in world coords (z = −plan y).
  - The camera y must include terrain height. The build box renders on the CPU, about 10 min per shot.
- **Other demo modes:** `--demo=drive|walk|fly|tour|bars`, `--at=x,z`, `--hour=H`.
- **Benchmark:** `godot --path . -- --benchmark=user://benchmark.json` (see `docs/PERF_BUDGETS.md`).

## 5. How the world is built (data pipeline)

`tools/build.sh city` runs `tools/pipeline/build_city.py`, which takes about 60 min on the 2-CPU, 7 GB build box. It turns the raw data into 200 m chunks.

**Coordinates:**
- Plan coords are metres around downtown (41.6528 N, −83.5379 W), with x east and y north.
- Game coords: x = plan x, z = −plan y, y = up. Chunks are 200 m; regions are 1 km.
- Origin rebasing keeps the player near (0,0,0). Anything stored across frames must use world coords.

### Sources (licences in `docs/DATA_SOURCES.md`)
- **OpenStreetMap (ODbL):** streets, water, parks, rail, POIs, footprints outside the county.
- **Lucas County:**
  - building footprints for the whole county;
  - AREIS records (wall material, storeys, year, land use, structure type);
  - parcel photos from the iCare viewer. The photos are reference only: only derived colours and labels ship.
- **USGS 3DEP LiDAR (public domain):**
  - every building's height, roof shape and stepped tiers;
  - exact downtown massing;
  - **terrain**: the 4 m DEM (`dem.py`); water pinned to 174.4 m; game datum 176 m.
- **Ohio OSIP aerial imagery:** the ground texture, plus lawn and grass detection.
- **Poly Haven (CC0)** materials and props. **MakeHuman / MPFB2 (CC0 assets)** people.

### What the build does
- **Roads:** buffered to real widths; sidewalks, lane lines, lamps; bridges with ramps; intersection controls.
- **Terrain:** buildings sit level on their lowest footprint ground point. Ground surfaces drape over the DEM. Water and bridges use absolute heights. `data/terrain.bin` (16 m grid, `scripts/terrain.gd`) lets the game query ground height.
- **Houses:**
  - county wall type and era give the siding or brick;
  - **real paint and trim colours** are measured from county photos (`house_looks.py`: segmentation and k-means);
  - **hand labels** for porch, dormer, shutters, roof and awning (`source_data/house_photo_labels.json`, 400+ houses);
  - 3D porches, steps, doors, windows and shutters (`tools/blender/house_parts.py`);
  - houses face the street of their address, with front walks;
  - height is capped by the storey count, because street trees inflate the LiDAR height.
- **Demolished houses:** county vacant-land records (land use 500, no building) remove about 1,000 houses that are gone in real life (`vacant_keys.py`).
- **Non-house buildings:** real wall colour from photos (`bld_looks_apply.py`).
  - Neighbourhood commercial strips (Main St, Starr Ave) are brick storefronts.
  - AREIS structure type 12 on a non-residential parcel is an old brick shop block, not a house (fixed 2026-11-21).
- **Landmark replicas:** hand-specced buildings made in Blender (`tools/blender/replica.py`, specs in `tools/blender/replicas/<key>/spec.json`).
  - The exact LiDAR footprint and tiers come from `replica_add.py`.
  - Features: facades, arches, cornices, porticos, domes, towers, rose windows, Gothic portals, chimneys, and now **theatre marquees with 3D letters**.
  - About 40 so far: downtown towers, the Courthouse, the Museum of Art, and 27 in East Toledo.
- **HLOD:** merged far-city meshes, one per 400 m region.

### Character creator (in progress, started 2026-11-21)
The owner wants a Sims/NBA 2K-style creator: full skeleton, detailed face sliders, and "send a photo, get a good-looking model of that person". **Standing rule: when the owner asks for character models, build them with this creator on the MPFB2 add-on.**

**MPFB2 setup:**
- MPFB2 (MakeHuman Plugin For Blender, GPL code, CC0 assets) is installed as a Blender extension for the bpy module: the folder `~/.config/blender/5.0/extensions/user_default/mpfb` links to `dl/mpfb_src/mpfb2/src/mpfb`.
- The asset packs are linked into MPFB's user data dir.

**`tools/blender/charcreator/make_character.py SPEC.json OUT.glb [PREVIEW.png]`** builds a full person.
- **Spec fields:**
  - `phenotype`: gender, age, muscle, weight, height, ethnicity mix;
  - face and body `targets`: 272 face sliders are available;
  - `skin`, `eyes`, `eyebrows`, `hair`, `clothes`;
  - `hair_color`;
  - `style`;
  - `fit`.
- **Output:** the `game_engine` rig, exported as `.glb`.
- **Preview:** a front face, three-quarter and full-body render. `QUICK=1` renders the front face only.

**Photo to face:**
1. `tools/pipeline/face_landmarks.py PHOTO OUT.json` runs the MediaPipe Face Landmarker: 478 points, plus the skin, iris, hair, brow and lip colours, and the photo's expression.
2. `charcreator/facefit.py` maps MediaPipe's 468 points to MakeHuman vertices. The map is cached in `mp_to_mh.json`.
3. It then solves about 45 curated symmetric face sliders. This is a signed two-pass bounded least-squares solve with pose alignment.
4. It can project the photo onto the skin texture.

**Stylized mode** (`style`: `exaggerate`, `head_scale`, `hand_scale`, `painted`; `charcreator/stylize.py`) applies the Bully-like look: caricatured sliders, bigger head and hands, and painted skin re-toned to the photo.

**Known issues (2026-11-21):**
- A pale blob shows below the lower lip after fitting. Being diagnosed; it is suspected to be the teeth proxy (`"teeth": false` spec option added to test).
- Likeness is still weak.
- The skin re-tone runs too orange in warm photos.
- Photo projection showed lighting seams.
- Not yet: a Blender UI panel with sliders, and plugging creator models into the game.

**Test photos:** GAN-generated faces (not real people) from the NVIDIA StyleGAN2 repo, in `dl/facetest/`.

### GLASS CITY's own human system (owner, 2026-11-21: "don't use MakeHuman, make your own")
**PAUSED (owner, 2026-11-21 16:35):** "That's nowhere near close, let's ignore this for a little and go back to the city." The SDF head below is the starting point when it resumes. The owner judged the clay head far from the Bully reference look.
All game characters, NPCs and other humans will come from our own generator in `tools/blender/glasshuman/`, in Bully style. **MakeHuman/MPFB2 is no longer used for game people**; the older creator code stays only as reference.
- **Head** (`glasshuman/head.py`, in progress): parametric and built from scratch as a signed-distance field (SDF) of sculpting primitives blended with smooth unions:
  - cranium, forehead, face mass, jaw (ellipsoid ↔ rounded box for `jaw_square`), jaw angles, chin, cheekbones, cheeks, muzzle;
  - brow shelf plus glabella;
  - eye sockets with eyelids (an almond opening cut into a shell round the eyeball);
  - nose (bridge capsules, bulb, wings, nostril holes);
  - full lips with a shallow slit (the line itself is painted), philtrum, the groove under the lower lip;
  - neck.

  A fixed set of rays (dense over the face) is shot inward and each stops at the surface, so every face has identical topology. A slider's blend shape is `head(P + 1) - head(P)`.
  - About 45 named face parameters.
  - Preview with `bpyenv/bin/python tools/blender/glasshuman/preview_head.py -- OUT.png [param=value ...]`, which renders front, ¾ and side views.
- **Style target** (owner's reference screenshots, 2026-11-21): the 2006–2008 Rockstar look:
  - compact, rounded heads;
  - small, deep-set, squinting eyes under a heavy low brow;
  - broad, fleshy noses and full lower lips;
  - wide soft jaws and thick necks;
  - expression baked into the face (scowls);
  - smooth, simple forms, with detail **painted** into the skin: freckles, ruddy cheeks, painted shading under the brow, lip colour, stubble;
  - big hands and chunky forearms.
  
  We match the look but never copy a specific character.
- **Planned:**
  - body and hands built the same way, with Bully proportions;
  - our own skeleton with the game's bone names and computed skin weights;
  - painted skin laid out in the head's angular UV space;
  - 500 face presets per gender, as slider vectors plus skin tone, eye colour and brows;
  - 200 generated hairstyles;
  - our own clothes.
- **Animations stay:** the CMU motion capture (`tools/blender/rigged_people.py` retarget) is free mocap data, not MakeHuman. The owner approved keeping it.
